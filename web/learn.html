Understand the molecule before the model.

A chapter-style primer for PROTAC anatomy, why segmentation matters, and what “reassembly” means chemically.

What is a PROTAC?

A PROTAC (PROteolysis TArgeting Chimera) is a three-part molecule that tags a disease-related protein for destruction by the cell’s ubiquitin–proteasome system.

Instead of only inhibiting a protein, it recruits an E3 ligase so the target gets ubiquitinated and degraded.

Three parts

Warhead
Linker
E3

Color language used across Yashi: coral = warhead, blue = linker, mint = E3.

Warhead binds the target protein.

Linker bridges the two ligands and controls geometry.

E3 ligand recruits the ubiquitin machinery.

Why segment?

Public databases often give only the full PROTAC SMILES. Designers still want to know which atoms belong to which part.

  • Reuse warheads / E3s in new designs
  • Study linker chemistry separately
  • Train models that generalize to novel combinations

Yashi frames this as atom-level segmentation on a molecular graph.

Reassembly check

If you cut the molecule at warhead–linker and linker–E3 borders, you should get exactly three chemically valid fragments that cover all atoms.

That “reassembly” idea is used both as a label filter (notebook 01) and as an evaluation metric.

Skill tip Thinking in constraints (not only accuracy) is how chemistry-aware ML papers survive review.
